# Oral-Defense Notes

**Status:** Preparation prompts; expand using the actual implementation.

Each team member should be able to answer:

1. What problem does the prototype solve, and what is explicitly outside its scope?
2. Why is logic programming suitable for this problem?
3. What does each major fact and predicate represent?
4. How does a query derive a candidate match?
5. Where does unification bind variables?
6. How does backtracking produce alternative candidates?
7. Why is the recursive predicate meaningful rather than decorative?
8. How are duplicate and unknown symptoms handled?
9. How is overlap calculated, and why is it not a probability or diagnosis?
10. How are ties and no-match cases preserved?
11. If negation as failure or cut is used, why is it logically appropriate?
12. Which tests were actually executed, and what defects were corrected?
13. Which sources support the knowledge, and what simplifications were made?
14. What are the limitations and safest feasible future improvements?
15. How does the submission satisfy every stage and the video requirement?

Never memorize an answer that does not match the verified application.
